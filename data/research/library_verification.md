# Verified against the research library

Checked 29 October 2026 on the owner's own machine, against the scanned sources held in the library
(which a cloud session cannot reach). Companion to `quote_verification.md`, which covers the web-only
sources.

Every item below was read from the page image or the page's own text, not from a catalogue entry.

## 1. The 1550 *Antiquitez* is the 1550 *Antiquitez*

The first edition was uploaded to the library on 29 October (Gallica `ark:/12148/bpt6k1527257k`, 459
scan pages). Gallica's own cover sheet says 1550, but that is the catalogue, which is exactly what
this project has twice been burnt by. Read from the book's own title page instead (scan p. 13):

> LES ANTIQVITEZ, HISTOIRES ET SINGVLARITEZ de PARIS, ville capitale du Royaume de France.
> Auec priuilege du Roy pour vi. ans.
> **1550.**
> A PARIS, Au Palais, en la boutique de Gilles Corrozet.

with the printer's device (motto *ALTVM LABORE CONSCENDIMVS*) and the BnF Réserve des imprimés stamp
no. 3156. Confirmed.

**This also confirms, from a primary source, where Corrozet sold books:** *Au Palais, en la boutique
de Gilles Corrozet* — the Palais de la Cité, not the rue Montorgueil. The about page already says
"bookseller at the Palais"; it now rests on his own imprint rather than on a reference work.

⚠ Corrozet's shop at the Palais and the publishers' shop "au chef Sainct-Denys" in the rue
Montorgueil are **two different shops**. The open research question is about the second.

## 2. The dedicatee of the 1550 edition is the prévôt des marchands — and he is in our Registres

Scan p. 17 of the 1550 *Antiquitez*:

> A NOBLE SEIGNEVR, Monseigneur Claude Guiot, secretaire & cõseiller du Roy nostre Sire, contrerolleur
> de l'audience de la chãcelerie de France, **& preuost des marchans de la ville de Paris**, Gilles
> Corrozet dedie ce present Liure.

The same man appears in *Registres des délibérations du bureau de la Ville de Paris*, tome 3
(1539–1552), scan p. 228, under 24 December 1549:

> … a esté conciud et advisé que mons' m° **Claude Guyot, Prevost des Marchans**, et sire Guillaume
> Pommereu, Eschevin de lad. Ville, yront par devers le Roy et son Conseil, pour demander
> l'assignation et recouvrement de l'artillerye prestée par lad. Ville au feu Roy.

and returning on 13 January 1550. So the book's dedicatee was the city's chief magistrate in the very
months the book was being printed — two sources in the library meeting at one person. (Spelling
*Guiot* / *Guyot* varies between the two; both are the same office and the same date range.)

## 3. The *Fleur* page numbers on the site are right; our own log was the loose one

`web/texts/index.html` cites the poem in the Lacroix 1874 reprint at **pp. 64–68**. `LOG.md` and the
project memory say "pp. 102–106". Both are true of different things, and the site has the right one:

- scan / Gallica viewer page **102** carries the printed page number **64**, with the running head
  LES ANTIQUITEZ. The offset is 38, as suspected but not previously read.
- That page holds the end of « Le nom et surnom de laucteur » (the fourteen-line acrostic) and the
  opening of « Louenges de Paris faictes par ledict aucteur » — the long poem that is on the map.

So **cite printed pages 64–68**, and treat the 102–106 in the log as viewer pages.

## 4. Cousin on Nicolas Prévost: the house *belonged* to him

Cousin 1875, scan p. 61, in full:

> Ces planches datent de 1572 ou environ; nous n'y retrouvons plus la signature d'Olivier Truschet …
> mais Germain Hoyau en a signé plusieurs en compagnie de Mathurin Nicolas son nouvel associé à
> l'enseigne du Bon Pasteur. **La maison du Chef Saint-Denis, sans doute liquidée après la mort de
> Truschet, appartient alors à Nicolas Prévost.** Elle a pour voisines — toujours dans la même rue
> Montorgueil: la maison de l'Échiquier … la Corne de Cerf, à Marin Boussy, etc., etc., **toute une
> colonie d'imagiers**.

The about page said Prévost "printed there", cited to Cousin. Cousin says the house belonged to him.
Corrected in en/fr/sv on 29 October. Cousin *does* support the street as a colony of image-makers,
so that half stands.

Note also what Cousin does and does not say about Truschet's death: "**sans doute** liquidée après la
mort de Truschet" is Cousin's own inference, hedged, from the signatures disappearing by about 1572 —
not a document. The site's separate "by 1575 Truschet was dead", cited to [9], does not rest on
Cousin and is still unverified.
