# The print shop "au Chef Sainct-Denys", rue Montorgueil

Research note, 2026-10-26 (cloud session, branch `print-shop-research`).

**Status: open** — listed in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` ("Research questions"); summary on the site at https://paris1550.vercel.app/#shop (section "Where was the shop?"). Add new findings here.

## Question

The publishers' notice on the map reads: *"A Paris, par Olivier Truschet, & Germain Hoyau, demourans en
la Rue de Montorgueil, au Chef sainct Denys."* Where was the house at the sign of the Chef Saint-Denis,
in terms of today's rue Montorgueil?

## Short answer

**House not found.** No source I could reach places the Chef Saint-Denis at a present-day street
number, block, side of the street or cross-street. What can be said:

1. The map itself names the southern stretch separately ("R. Conte Dartois", Cousin's table), so on the
   publishers' own sheet "rue de Montorgueil" meant the street **north of the rue Mauconseil**. The shop
   was somewhere on today's rue Montorgueil between the rue Mauconseil and the rue Saint-Sauveur, or on
   today's rue des Petits-Carreaux, which was still counted as part of the rue Montorgueil until the early
   17th century. (Medium confidence: it assumes the notice uses the same street names as the map.)
2. The house is well attested as a print shop over about twenty years: Truschet and Hoyau there c. 1550–53,
   Nicolas Prévost there in 1571 (Lepape, extract). It was one of a cluster of image-makers' houses
   (Bon Pasteur, Corne de Daim, Échiquier, Espinette, Cuillère…), none of which I found matched to a
   modern address either.

## Findings

### 1. Where "rue Montorgueil" ran in 1550

**Southern end — confirmed.** The stretch from the rue Montmartre (Pointe Saint-Eustache) to the rue
Mauconseil was the rue au Comte-d'Artois (also rue de la Comtesse-d'Artois, de la Porte au Comte),
named after the counts of Artois' hôtel and the "porte au Comte d'Artois", a postern of the Philippe
Auguste wall; the gate was ordered demolished on **17 December 1498**; the street was joined to the rue
Montorgueil in **1792** (LAZARE, djvu p. 470, **read**: « la Ville ordonna, le 17 décembre 1498, de la
démolir »; « En 1792 la rue Comtesse-d'Artois fut confondue avec la rue Montorgueil ». Lazare also
confirms the southern stretch ran from the Pointe-Saint-Eustache **to the rue Mauconseil**, and names
Robert II, comte d'Artois, whose hôtel stood between the rues Pavée et Mauconseil). **New, and stronger than the register:** the 1550 map
itself labels it separately: Cousin's table has "COMTE-D'ARTOIS (rue au) | R. Conte Dartois | VI,1" as
a distinct entry from "MONTORGUEIL (rue de) | VI,1; V,3" (COUSIN-T, read in full; project data:
`streets.csv` L0378 vs L0381). So on the publishers' own sheet the shop's street began at the rue
Mauconseil.

**Northern end — settled.** The Archives nationales register (**read**) says: « Elle existait au XIIIe
siècle. Jusqu'au début du XVIIe siècle, elle a été confondue avec la rue Montorgueil. En 1637, la partie
voisine de la rue Poissonnière était appelée rue des Boucheries. » French Wikipedia (**read**) agrees and
adds that the form « rue des Petitz Carneaulx » is first found in a manuscript of **1636**. So in 1550 the
name very probably covered today's rue des Petits-Carreaux as well, i.e. it ran up to the Charles V wall,
whose ditch is followed by today's rue d'Aboukir (WP, extract).

**No Porte Montorgueil in the Charles V wall.** French Wikipedia (**read**): « L'enceinte ne comprend à
l'origine que six portes » — Saint-Honoré, Montmartre, Saint-Denis, Saint-Martin, Temple,
Saint-Antoine — none at Montorgueil. (A seventh, the porte Neuve, opened in 1536.) The porte Montmartre
stood where the rue Montmartre crosses today's rue d'Aboukir. The only "Montorgueil" gate in the sources is the 13th-century postern of the
Philippe Auguste wall at the *southern* end (= porte au Comte d'Artois). I did not establish whether the
street ended blind at the wall in 1550 or passed it by a minor opening; not found.

### 2. The house at the Chef Saint-Denis

- 1550s: Truschet and Hoyau "demourans en la rue de Montorgueil, au chef Sainct-Denys" (the map; COUSIN).
- **Cousin, printed p. 53 — confirmed, read in this repository** (`cousin_1875_notice.txt:315–321`):
  "Ces planches datent de 1572 ou environ; [...] La maison du Chef Saint-Denis, sans doute liquidée après
  la mort de Truschet, appartient alors à Nicolas Prévost." Note what Cousin actually claims: the house
  **belonged to** Prévost around 1572. He does not say Prévost printed there, and he gives no year for
  Truschet's death.
- **LEPAPE, chapter "Prévost" — read, confirmed**: « Nicolas Prévost (vers 1535/1540-vers 1599) … Les
  actes le désignent comme peintre en papier, imprimeur d'histoires et imagier. Il est installé rue
  Montorgueil au Chef Saint-Denis, **où il se trouvait en 1571** (taxé 60 sols au don de 300 000 livres,
  voir *Dictionnaire des éditeurs…*, p. 267-268). … On ne sait quelles étaient les modalités
  d'occupation de cette maison, **qui était en 1553 le lieu d'édition de Truschet et Hoyau pour le Plan
  de Paris**. » So 1571 rests on a tax roll, which is firmer than Cousin's undated prints.
- **Lepape dates the plan 1553 four times over** — the Prévost chapter above; Truschet's biography
  (p. 306-308), « où il publia en 1553 avec Germain Hoyau le fameux plan de Paris (cat. 228) »; Hoyau's
  (p. 136-168), « un plan de Paris (aujourd'hui conservé à Bâle), que l'on date de 1553 »; and the list
  of signs (p. 345). **But in none of them does she say which document carries the date** — « que l'on
  date de 1553 » reads as received opinion. And the table of contents has **no** entry "Germain Hoyau au
  Chef Saint-Denis en 1553": it lists only "Les Hoyau et Nicolas" and "Truschet". That earlier note was
  wrong.
- **How long they were there** (LEPAPE, signs list p. 345, **read**): « Au Chef Saint-Denis Germain Hoyau
  et Olivier Truschet y publièrent le Plan de Paris en 1553, **jusqu'à 1563 au plus tard pour Germain
  Hoyau, et avant (1559 ?) pour Olivier Truschet.** Nicolas Prévost publia ses pl. à c[ette adresse] ».
  So the house was theirs for a shorter span than “c. 1550–53” suggests at one end and longer at the
  other, and it passed to Prévost, not straight from them.
- No source located the house more closely than "rue Montorgueil". **Still not found**, including in
  Lepape's list of known signs, which is the likeliest place for it.

The image-makers were parishioners of **Saint-Eustache** (Fonteney's daughter Jeanne baptised there
**29 May 1579**: LEPAPE 2434, **read**; her godfather was the imagier Jean Boussy). Hofmann adds that by

partnership agreements” (HOC ch. 53, p. 1572).

### 4. Truschet and Hoyau: other solid facts

- **Trades.** LEPAPE (**read**, p. 306-308): Truschet « est désigné dans les actes comme imagier, peintre
  en papier et dominotier ». Cousin (read in full) found they were not booksellers (checked against
  Lottin). HOC ch. 48, p. 1500 (**read**) is the "other way round" source the about page mentions, and
  its wording is now exact: « the map of Paris **drafted by Germain Hoyau (ca. 1525–83), imagier en
  papier (producer of prints) and drawer, and engraved by Olivier Truschet** », following Dérens. French
  Wikipedia has it the other way (« le dessinateur Olivier Truschet et le graveur Germain Hoyau »). The
  **Institut Paris Région page does not take sides**: it says only « du nom de son dessinateur et de son
  graveur », without saying which is which.
- **Other works by Truschet** (LEPAPE 2395, dated-prints list p. 349-350, **read**): « 1559 *Carte de la
  cité de Guînes*, par Olivier Truschet. 1560 *Carte de France, d'Allemagne et d'Italie* de Jean Jolivet,
  par Olivier Truschet. ***Monstre découvert dans le Berry***, par Olivier Truschet. » — a **fourth** work
  we had missed, published with Richard Breton (cat. 592; the Jolivet map is cat. 591), both from the Bon
  Pasteur. HOC ch. 53, p. 1572 (**read**): the Guînes map (ca. 1559) was « copied from a manuscript by
  Nicolas de Nicolay », and Truschet also published *Description de la haulte et basse Picardye*
  (ca. 1559–60), **attributed to Jolivet**, and *Nouvelle description des Gaules* (1560) by Jolivet — a
  different 1560 Jolivet title from Lepape's. So Truschet was a map publisher beyond the Paris plan.
- **Hoyau (?–1583).** Lepape heads his biography « Germain Hoyau ( ?-1583) » and the signs list says
  « mort en 1583 »; the Louvre credits him « vers 1518-1583 » after Lepape, HOC « ca. 1525–83 ». **So the
  old bracket 1572–1585 collapses to 1583.** 24 Aug. 1564: Gilles Godet — a Paris imagier settled in
  London — sold Hoyau, Desprez and Mathurin Nicolas « douze pl. de bois esquelles sont gravés les douze
  moys, servant pour l'imprimerie, pour 200 livres tournois » (Louvre cl020519735, **read**, citing
  Lepape no. 164). Jeanne Hoyau, « qui serait selon Marianne Grivel la sœur de Germain Hoyau », married
  François Gence — though the same chapter calls Hoyau the « grand-père maternel » of her son, which
  would make her his daughter; **Lepape is inconsistent here**. He was godfather to that grandson Germain
  Gence, baptised **19 Nov. 1572**. His widow died in **1585**. The index entry « Veuve de Mathurin
  Nicolas : voir Hoyau (Catherine) » is now explained: **Catherine was Hoyau's own daughter**, widow of
  his partner Nicolas (d. 1580), then wife of Roland Guérard. 15 March 1560: Hoyau, Nicolas and Nicolas
  Lefebvre obtained a privilege for maps of Rhodes, Crete and Madagascar (HOC ch. 53).
- **Truschet (?–vers 1561/1564).** LEPAPE heads his biography « Olivier Truschet ( ?-vers 1561/1564) »
  and the signs list says « mort entre 1561 et 1564 ». **"Dead by 1575" is true but more than a decade
  too loose**; that date comes only from Grivel's note that his name in the 1575 censier is an error.
  He is absent from the c. 1572 prints (COUSIN). His widow was **Thomasse Laloze**, who remarried; his
  sons Germain (bapt. 7 July 1551, godfathers Germain Hoyau and Jean III Leclerc) and Olivier (bapt.
  11 Dec. 1553, godfather François Desprez).

## Confidence

- Southern limit (rue Mauconseil) of the 1550 rue Montorgueil: **high** (Lazare read; register; the map's own label).
- Petits-Carreaux included in 1550: **high** (AN register and French Wikipedia both read, and they agree).
- Chef Saint-Denis = a print shop continuously from 1553 to 1571: **high** (Cousin and Lepape both read).
- Present-day location of the Chef Saint-Denis: **not found**. No street number should be published.

## Where to look next (archive access needed)

1. **Read Lepape 2016 in full.** The opening text of every chapter is served publicly and has now been
   read (above); the rest is behind library access. Still wanted: whether any notarial act locates a
   house by its neighbours ("tenant d'une part à… aboutissant à…"), and **what document carries the
   1553 date** — she gives the date four times and never cites a source for it.
2. **Grivel 2008 and 2015** (*Documents d'histoire parisienne* 9 and 17; HAL halshs-00602821). Notarial
   sales of shares of the Bon Pasteur (1563, 1566) normally give abutments, which would place the Bon
   Pasteur relative to its neighbours and possibly to the Chef Saint-Denis.
3. **The bishop's and archbishop's censiers — now with a cote.** Lepape records Hoyau's sixth of the Bon
   Pasteur in the « registres d'ensaisinement de l'évêché de Paris » at **AN, S* 1214**, and the 1575
   censier via Grivel 2008 p. 21. That is the chain to follow. These registers (Archives nationales,
   series S) list houses in street order with signs and
   owners, and can be chained forward to the 1786 terrier whose parcel plans Brette published (BRETTE,
   1906). That chain is the standard way (Berty's method) to put a 16th-century sign on a modern parcel.
4. **Minutier central** (AN, MC): the Jean I·er· Boussy inventory of **2 Nov. 1547** (BOUSSY-IAD; his
   marriage contract is MC, CXXII, 1156) and the Mathonière acts (1557, 1565; the 1598 inventory is
   **MC XV, 48**) — inventories après décès describe the house room by room and often its abutments.
   Catherine Grodecki, *Documents du Minutier central des notaires de Paris. Histoire de l'art au XVIe
   siècle (1540–1600)*, 2 vols (1985–86) indexes such acts by name; look up Truschet, Hoyau, Prévost,
   Boussy, Mathonière, Bonnemer.
5. **Saint-Eustache parish registers**: the originals burned in 1871; Lepape's baptisms presumably come
   from the Laborde index (BnF, fichier Laborde) — worth a name search for Truschet.

## Ready-to-paste text for "Who made it"

Source numbers: `[1]` = Cousin 1875 notice (existing), `[2]` = Cousin's table (existing), `[9]` = Lepape,
"Les Desprez" (existing). New: `[A]` Lepape 2016 (whole book / "Prévost" chapter), `[B]` Grivel 2008,
`[C]` Archives nationales street register (rue des Petits-Carreaux; rue au Comte-d'Artois). Full entries
below. **The † marks are gone: all of them were checked in Lepape on 29 Oct 2026 and confirmed.** Two
death dates in the old text have been corrected at the same time (Truschet, Hoyau).

### English

The address on the map is "in the rue Montorgueil, at the sign of the Head of Saint Denis". In 1550 that
name did not cover the whole of today's street: the stretch south of the rue Mauconseil was the rue au
Comte-d'Artois, and the map labels it so; it joined the rue Montorgueil only in 1792
[2][C]. To the north the name still took in today's rue des Petits-Carreaux, a separate street only in the
17th century [C]. The shop stood somewhere on that stretch, between the rue Mauconseil and the city wall. Exactly where is not known: no study we found ties the sign to a present-day house.

The house stayed in the trade. By 1571 the image-maker Nicolas Prévost was at the Head of Saint Denis,
where the tax roll of that year found him [A], and the neighbours were image-makers too: the Chessboard,
the Deer's Horn, the Spinet, the Stag's Horn [1]. Truschet also published maps of Guînes (1559) and of
France, Germany and Italy (1560) [A]; he died between 1561 and 1564 [A]. Hoyau bought a sixth of the
house of the Good Shepherd in the same street in 1563 and another in 1566, and died in 1583 [9][A].

### Français

L'adresse portée sur le plan est « en la rue de Montorgueil, au Chef Saint-Denis ». En 1550 ce nom ne
couvrait pas toute la rue actuelle : au sud de la rue Mauconseil, c'était la rue au Comte-d'Artois, que le
plan nomme ainsi, réunie à la rue Montorgueil seulement en 1792 [2][C]. Au nord, en revanche, le nom
englobait encore l'actuelle rue des Petits-Carreaux, qui n'est devenue une rue distincte qu'au XVIIe siècle
[C]. La boutique se trouvait quelque part sur ce tronçon, entre la rue Mauconseil et l'enceinte. On ne sait pas où exactement : aucune étude que nous ayons trouvée ne rattache l'enseigne à une
maison d'aujourd'hui.

La maison resta dans le métier. En 1571 l'imagier Nicolas Prévost était au Chef Saint-Denis, où le rôle
d'impôt de cette année-là le trouve [A], et les voisins étaient eux aussi imagiers : l'Échiquier, la
Corne de Daim, l'Espinette, la Corne de Cerf [1]. Truschet a aussi publié une carte de Guînes (1559) et
une carte de France, d'Allemagne et d'Italie (1560) [A] ; il mourut entre 1561 et 1564 [A]. Hoyau acheta
un sixième de la maison du Bon Pasteur, dans la même rue, en 1563, un autre en 1566, et mourut en
1583 [9][A].

### Svenska

Adressen på kartan är ”i rue de Montorgueil, vid skylten Sankt Denis huvud”. År 1550 gällde namnet inte
hela dagens gata: sträckan söder om rue Mauconseil hette rue au Comte-d'Artois, och kartan kallar den så;
den slogs ihop med rue Montorgueil först 1792 [2][C]. Norrut omfattade namnet däremot fortfarande
dagens rue des Petits-Carreaux, som blev en egen gata först på 1600-talet [C]. Någonstans på den
sträckan, mellan rue Mauconseil och stadsmuren, låg verkstaden. Exakt var vet man inte:
ingen undersökning vi har hittat knyter skylten till ett hus som står i dag.

Huset förblev en bildtryckarverkstad. År 1571 fanns bildmakaren Nicolas Prévost vid Sankt Denis huvud,
där årets skattelängd upptar honom [A], och grannarna var också bildmakare: Schackbrädet,
Dovhjortshornet, Spinetten, Kronhjortshornet [1]. Truschet gav också ut kartor över Guînes (1559) och
över Frankrike, Tyskland och Italien (1560) [A]; han dog mellan 1561 och 1564 [A]. Hoyau köpte en sjättedel
av huset Den gode herden på samma gata 1563 och ytterligare en 1566, och dog 1583 [9][A].

Notes on the wording:
- The sources say only that the Bon Pasteur was in the *same street*; don't write "next door" or
  "a few doors away".
- No length is given for the stretch Mauconseil → city wall; measure it on the project's street
  geometry before adding a figure.
- "Spinet" for *l'Espinette*: an *espinette* is a small spinet (keyboard instrument), but it could also be
  a thorn-bush (*épine*). If Lepape or Grivel don't explain it, leave the name in French.
- Prévost's 1571 date and Truschet's 1559/1560 maps were marked †; **all were confirmed in Lepape on
  29 Oct 2026** and the marks are gone. Note that Cousin says only that the house *belonged to* Prévost
  around 1572 (« appartient alors à Nicolas Prévost »); it is Lepape, from the 1571 tax roll, who puts
  him *there*. Cite Lepape, not Cousin, for 1571.
- Truschet's and Hoyau's death dates in the earlier draft ("dead by 1575", "alive in 1572") were far
  looser than the sources allow: Lepape gives **1561/1564** and **1583**.
