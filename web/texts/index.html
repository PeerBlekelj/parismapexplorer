The texts on the map

Below the city, the map carries a poem of 108 lines in praise of Paris, set in two framed panels, and a notice from its publishers. Here they are as printed, with an English translation line by line.

A poem in praise of Paris

The poem starts in the large panel at the lower left and carries on in the smaller panel in the middle, linked by the catchword “Le sainct” at the foot of the first. It is anonymous. It runs through the city's legendary founding, its setting, its castles and great houses, its walls, bridges and churches, its courts, its university and its trade.

1Gentilz lecteurs amateurs d'escripture,Gentle readers who love the written word,
2Ioyeulx espritz regardez la staturejoyful minds, behold the stature,
3Le bastiment & la fondationthe building and the founding,
4L'accroissement & l'augmentation,the growth and the increase,
5Et la façon comment Paris la villeand the way in which the town of Paris
6S'est augmentée en matiere ciuille:has grown as a city:
7Considerez la sienne antiquitéconsider its antiquity,
8Ou maintz cas sont de singularité.in which many things are remarkable.
9Regardez bien tous ces beaux edificesLook well at all these fine buildings,
10Recongnoissez ses louenges propicesrecognise the praises it deserves,
11Ou on comprent sa valeur & noblessein which its worth and nobility are found,
12Son hault estat: sa doulce gentillesse,its high estate, its gentle grace,
13Et tous les biens qu'on peult en veritéand all the good that can truly
14Totallement dire d'une cité.be said of a city.
15Noble cité & ville tant exquiseNoble city and town so exquisite,
16Dicte Paris de toutes gens requisecalled Paris, sought out by all people,
17Peult auoir loz & deseruir la gloiremay have praise and deserve the glory
18De tout honneur pour l'antique memoireof every honour, for the ancient memory
19De son premier & noble instaurateur,of its first and noble founder,
20S'il est ainsi que Paris fondateurwhether Paris was its founder,
21Ou s'ainsi est qu'auec puissant arroyor whether, with a mighty host,
22Herculez Grec ait faict fondationHercules the Greek founded
23De la cité par une nationthe city with a people
24De son pays, ou si la gent Troyenneof his own country, or whether the Trojan race
25Ont, la cité dicte parisienne,made, built and brought into being
26Faicte & bastie, & mise en son essencethe city called Parisian.
27Ceulx cy nommez de treshaulte excellence:Those named here were of the highest excellence
28Furent iadis, & de noblesse antique,in times past, and of ancient nobility;
29Mesmement ceulx de source Dardaniqueindeed those of the line of Dardanus
30Furent trespreux, & les plus ancienswere most valiant, and the most ancient
31Qui furent onc es pays Asiens.that ever were in the lands of Asia.
32Paris aussi eust couronne royalleParis too wore a royal crown
33Et fut le Roy de France la loyalle,and was king of loyal France,
34Dont si l'un d'eulx la faicte & instauréeso if one of them made and founded it,
35Estre ell' en doibt prisée & honorée,it ought to be prized and honoured for it.
36Pareillement la sienne antiquitéIts antiquity, likewise,
37Est de long temps, car à la veritégoes back a long way, for in truth
38Huyt cens trente ans auant que Dieu souffriteight hundred and thirty years before God suffered
39Tresdure mort, & à la croix s'offrita cruel death and offered himself on the cross,
40Paris auoit prins son commencementParis had taken its beginning
41Par les Troyens, & le sien fondement.and its foundation from the Trojans.
42Or est Paris en tresbel aer assise,Now Paris is set in very fine air;
43Entour soy a la riuiere propicearound it is the kindly river,
44Et fleuue doulx que l'on appelle Seine,the gentle stream called the Seine,
45Puis est remplie de tres belle fonteine:and it is full of very fine springs.
46De l'autre part sont les foretz tresplainesElsewhere are forests full
47De venaison, champaignes, boys, & plantes,of game, and fields, woods and plants,
48Terres portans les vignes tresplaisanteslands bearing most pleasant vines,
49Aultres aussy en tous bledz habondantes,others rich in every kind of grain.
50L'aer y est doulx, & la terre fertilleThe air is mild there, the soil fertile,
51Et en tous fruictz trescommode & utille.fit and useful for every kind of fruit.
52En elle aussi sont grans chasteaulx & toursIn it too are great castles and towers,
53Plus qu'il n'y a d'icy iusques à Tours.more than there are from here to Tours.
54Maisons d'honneur on voit dedens LuteceIn Lutetia one sees noble houses,
55Maint bastillon, & riche forteressemany a bastion and rich fortress,
56Comme le Louure, & la Bastille noblelike the Louvre and the noble Bastille,
57Dont telle n'a dedens Constantinoble.whose like Constantinople has not.
58Puis des Seigneurs les maisons de plaisanceThen the lords' houses of pleasure,
59Les grans logis ou prennent ample aisancethe great lodgings where they live in ample ease,
60Comme l'hostel de Bourbon, Villeroysuch as the Hôtel de Bourbon, Villeroy
61Dict chasteau pers, & la maison du Roycalled the 'château pers', and the King's house
62Pres le palays, lequel palays a bruytby the Palace, a palace renowned
63D'estre le mieulx en bel œuure construictas the finest and best-built work
64Qu'on veit iamais en la chrestienté,ever seen in Christendom,
65Pour sa grandeur, puis on voit à plantéfor its size; then one sees in plenty
66D'aultres logis pleins de beaulx edificesother lodgings, full of fine buildings
67Pour les bourgeoys & citoyens propices.fit for the burghers and citizens.
68Ceste ville à quatorze portes closeThis town is closed by fourteen gates
69Et haultz rampars, qui n'est pas peu de choseand high ramparts, which is no small thing;
70Profondz fossez tout alentour s'estendentdeep ditches stretch all around,
71Ou maintes eaues de toutes pars se rendent,into which many waters flow from every side.
72Puis apres sont cinq grans pontz pour passerThen there are five great bridges for crossing
73Par dessus l'eaue, aussy pour rapasser,over the water, and for crossing back,
74De la Cité, en l'uniuersitéfrom the Cité to the University.
75Auec ce sont maintes eglises bellesBesides, there are many fine churches,
76Temples diuins, monasteres, chappelles,holy temples, monasteries, chapels,
77En tresgrand nombre, on peult pareillementin very great number. One may likewise
78Ceste louer pour le beau parlement.praise this city for its fine Parlement:
79Le sainct senat ou sont les Conseilliersthe holy senate, where the councillors sit,
80Nombre six vingtz hommes tressinguliers,six score men of great distinction
81De grand sçauoir, qui tiennent leur officeand great learning, who hold their office
82De par le Roy, pour faire à tous iusticefrom the King, to do justice to all
83Sur les proces à la court intimezin the suits brought before the court.
84Aultres gens sont en iustice estimezOther men are respected in justice,
85Tenant le lieu du grant preuost royalholding the court of the great royal Provost,
86Pour ordonner leur iugement loyalto give their loyal judgement
87A toutes gens selon leur cause bonneto all, according to the merits of their cause.
88Apres y a mainte docte personneThen there is many a learned person
89Estudiant aux loix pour ce establizstudying the laws, appointed to that end,
90Et de sçauoir sont tresfort ennoblizand greatly ennobled by knowledge.
91Dedens Paris les sciences florissentWithin Paris the sciences flourish,
92Et gens sçauans en ce lieu resplandissentand learned men shine in this place
93Plus qu'en nul lieu, car Palas y octroyemore than anywhere, for Pallas grants,
94Autant ou plus qu'en Athenes ou Troyeas much as or more than in Athens or Troy,
95Le sien seiour: & les muses sçauantesher dwelling here; and the learned Muses
96Font en ce lieu leur demeure tenantes,make their lasting home in this place,
97Plus que iamais ne furent sur le mondmore than ever they did on the mount
98De Aonias, ou par elle semondof Aonia, where in former days
99Estoit iadis maint homme pour apprendre,she called many a man to learning.
100Artz & mestiers, apres nous fault comprendre,Arts and crafts, next, we must include,
101Desquelz il vient tresgrande utilitéfrom which comes very great profit
102Au lieu susdict aussy tranquilité,to the said place, and peace as well.
103Semblablement marchans de toutes guisesLikewise merchants of every kind
104Viennent illec pour toutes marchandisescome there to deal in every sort of ware,
105Distribuer, & tant de peuple abondeand so many people abound
106En cestuy lieu qu'il n'y a peuple au mondein this place that there is no people in the world
107Qui soit autant à chascun gratieuseso gracious to everyone
108Qu'est ceste cy, ny autant spatieuse.as this one, nor a city so spacious.

Notes

  • Paris, Hercules, the Trojans: the poem weighs the three founding legends told in the Renaissance. The favourite made the city's founder a Trojan prince called Paris, from the line of Dardanus, Troy's ancestor; another credited Hercules. None has any historical basis.
  • “Eight hundred and thirty years before God suffered”: that is, around 800 BC by the legend's reckoning.
  • Lutetia: the Roman name of Paris.
  • Villeroy, “château pers”: the line is hard to read with certainty; it seems to name the town house of the Neufville de Villeroy family. The meaning of “pers” (perhaps “blue-grey”, from its slate) is uncertain.
  • The King's house by the Palace: the royal palace on the Île de la Cité, which by then housed the Parlement; the Sainte-Chapelle stands in its courtyard.
  • Five great bridges: the Pont Notre-Dame, the Pont au Change and the Pont aux Meuniers on the right arm of the Seine, the Petit-Pont and the Pont Saint-Michel on the left: all on the map.
  • The holy senate: the Parlement of Paris, the highest court of the realm. “Six score” (120) is about right for its councillors in the 1550s.
  • The great royal Provost: the Prévôt de Paris, the king's judge, who sat at the Grand Châtelet.
  • Pallas, the Muses, Aonia: Pallas Athena, goddess of wisdom; the Muses lived on Mount Helicon in Aonia (Boeotia). The claim: Paris and its university outshine Athens.

The publishers' notice

1Icy est le vray pourtraict naturel de la ville, cité, vniuersité & Faubourgz de Paris, ou sont iustement figurées toutes les Rues & Ruelles correspondantes l'vne à l'autre, ainsi qui sont de present situées, qui sont en nombre deux cens quatre vingtz & sept. Pareillement sont figurées toutes les Eglises, & Monasteres, qui sont en nombre cent quatre. Aussy sont figurez tous les Colleges, qui sont en nombre quarante neuf. Et pour congnoistre icelles Rues, Ruelles, Eglises, Monasteres & Colleges, vous trouuerrez leurs noms escriptz à chascun sur son propre endroict. Comme plus amplement vous pouez voir cy dessus.Here is the true portrait from life of the town, city, university and suburbs of Paris, in which all the streets and lanes are accurately drawn, each meeting the next as they now lie: two hundred and eighty-seven of them. Likewise all the churches and monasteries are drawn: one hundred and four. All the colleges are drawn too: forty-nine. And so that you may know these streets, lanes, churches, monasteries and colleges, you will find the name of each written in its own place, as you can see in full above.
2A Paris, par Oliuier Truschet, & Germain Hoyau, demourans en la Rue de Montorgueil, au Chef sainct Denys.At Paris, by Olivier Truschet and Germain Hoyau, living in the rue Montorgueil, at the sign of the Head of Saint Denis.

Notes

  • The counts: the map names fewer streets, churches and colleges than it boasts; many are drawn but not labelled (see how the labels were read).
  • The publishers: Olivier Truschet was a printer and Germain Hoyau a woodcutter; nothing else is known of Hoyau. Their shop was in the rue Montorgueil, at a house with the sign of the head of Saint Denis.

Other words on the map

  • The title banner at the top: La ville, cité, université de Paris: “the town, the city and the university of Paris”, the three parts of Paris at the time: the Ville on the right bank, the Cité on the island, the Université on the left bank.
  • The winds and the quarters: blowing heads at the corners with Latin names: Subsolanus (the east wind) and Oriens (east) at the top, Auster (south), Favonius (the west wind) and Occidens (west) at the bottom, Septentriones (north) on the left. The map is printed with east at the top.
  • The coats of arms at the top: the arms of France (golden lilies on blue, under a crown) and the arms of Paris (the ship).
  • BIBL. PUBL. BASILIENSIS: the stamp of the public library of Basel, which holds the only known copy.

About this transcription

Transcribed from the Basel copy. The long s (ſ) is printed as s and the printer's abbreviations are written out; otherwise the spelling is kept as printed, including u for v and i for j. The translation follows the original line by line where the syntax allows, so a sentence sometimes runs across lines in a different order. The transcription and translation were made with Claude Code (see how this was built); corrections are welcome.
